# AfricMade interface design

Load before changing user-facing behavior, language, layout or navigation, and during AfricMade's broader usability review. Follow the accepted meaning and feature spec. Treat language, content hierarchy, interaction and layout as one problem: English is a meaning brief, not a template. Do not assume all African users, devices or buying habits are alike.

- Start with the user's real task. Make each action and outcome clear from its label; do not rely on an icon, hidden tooltip or slogan that needs decoding. Keep useful detail when it helps people compare products, understand listed availability or decide what to do.
- Keep internal record states, schema names and precision modes out of visitor-facing copy and accessibility names. Show the business name and actual city; name owner settings as actions people understand.
- Solve confusion in the interface before adding explanatory copy. Use clear page hierarchy, grouped related tasks, meaningful selected states, examples/previews, useful spacing and familiar interaction patterns so people can see where they are and what happens next. Text should name or clarify a real choice; it should not narrate an obvious control, compensate for scattered navigation, or turn the screen into instructions. Preserve concise text when it communicates a necessary outcome, eligibility, public visibility, privacy or risk.
- Treat a screen as a complete task flow, not a collection of controls. Identify its primary job, supporting choices, current state, next step, recovery path and way back. Put related records and actions together, reveal secondary detail when relevant, and make selection/save/loading/error states visible in the content they affect. Keep web, phone web and Expo coherent while using each platform's natural layout and navigation.
- Apply this to the whole product: visitors, business owners, staff and admins should be able to recognize where they are, find related work together, act without hunting, and recover without losing context. A repeated pattern of uncertainty is an information-architecture or interaction problem to fix across its affected screens, not a request to add more helper paragraphs to each screen.
- Author Amharic and Afaan Oromo for meaning and context. Prefer familiar task words and natural local order; avoid literal English structure, difficult invented terms, unexplained jargon and forced brevity. Keep merchant names, user data, quantities and identifiers literal. Catalog coverage is not proof of fluency or native-speaker review.
- Give each locale room. Let real text wrap; do not clip, overlap, shrink or hide meaning to preserve English dimensions. Check text scaling where relevant.
- On phones, protect content and controls from headers, drawers, floating actions, keyboards and safe areas. Keep actions reachable and easy to tap. Preserve content during loading, match skeletons to the affected region, and provide clear failure recovery.
- Reuse platform conventions without turning web and Expo into one screen. Keep state and Back behavior predictable; adapt to affected narrow, landscape and larger windows.

Pair interface work with mirtpage-browser-review. For usability audits, gather related problems across complete visitor, owner, staff and admin journeys before choosing fixes; distinguish information-architecture, interaction, visual hierarchy, copy/localization, accessibility, data and infrastructure issues. Fix shared layout or navigation causes once at their owning level, then inspect each affected screen; do not assume a copy edit resolves a structural problem. Exercise real journeys in affected web/Expo sizes and active locales. Inspect whether the task is apparent without a wall of instructions, then check labels, accessibility names, wrapping, keyboard/insets, loading/errors and return behavior; verify saves when relevant. Seek qualified language review for important launch copy when available and report when it has not happened. Batch related findings and record a source-bound agent visual pass/fail decision before expensive checks under DEP-002 S165.
